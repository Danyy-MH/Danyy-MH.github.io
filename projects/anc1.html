Adaptive Sliding Mode Control

This page summarizes the core equations of an adaptive sliding mode controller for trajectory tracking under parametric uncertainty and bounded disturbances. The controller combines a sliding manifold for robust error dynamics with an online adaptation law that updates the uncertain parameters in real time.

For the tracking error \( e = x - x_d \), a standard first-order sliding surface is defined as

\[ s = \dot{e} + \Lambda e \]

where \( \Lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n} \) is a positive definite design matrix. Assuming the dynamics can be written as

\[ M(x)\ddot{x} + C(x,\dot{x})\dot{x} + g(x) = Y(x,\dot{x},x_d,\dot{x}_d,\ddot{x}_d)\theta + d(t) + u \]

the adaptive sliding mode control law is chosen as

\[ u = -Y(x,\dot{x},x_d,\dot{x}_d,\ddot{x}_d)\hat{\theta} - K s - \hat{\rho}\,\mathrm{sat}\!\left(\frac{s}{\phi}\right) \]

where \( K \) is a positive definite gain matrix, \( \hat{\theta} \) is the online estimate of the uncertain parameter vector \( \theta \), and \( \hat{\rho} \) adapts the robust switching gain. The saturation function reduces chattering inside the boundary layer \( \phi > 0 \).

A common parameter adaptation law is

\[ \dot{\hat{\theta}} = \Gamma Y^\top s \]

with \( \Gamma \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times p} \) symmetric positive definite. The switching gain can also be adapted online through

\[ \dot{\hat{\rho}} = \gamma_{\rho}\|s\|, \qquad \hat{\rho}(0) > 0 \]

To study stability, define the Lyapunov candidate

\[ V = \frac{1}{2}s^\top M(x)s + \frac{1}{2}\tilde{\theta}^\top \Gamma^{-1}\tilde{\theta} + \frac{1}{2\gamma_{\rho}}\tilde{\rho}^{\,2} \]

where \( \tilde{\theta} = \theta - \hat{\theta} \) and \( \tilde{\rho} = \rho - \hat{\rho} \). Under the preceding control and adaptation laws, the closed-loop derivative satisfies

\[ \dot{V} \le - s^\top K s - \left(\hat{\rho} - \|d(t)\|\right)\|s\| \]

which implies boundedness of all closed-loop signals and convergence of the tracking error to a neighborhood of the sliding manifold. With sufficiently large gains and disturbance estimation, the manifold can be made arbitrarily tight for high-accuracy tracking.
